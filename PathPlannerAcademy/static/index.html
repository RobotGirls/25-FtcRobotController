    PATH PLANNER ACADEMY GRADUATE

    You taught Buzzy how to find its way!

    You planned, tested, repaired, and drove a real Pure Pursuit simulation. That is a big piece of robot navigation—but it is not the whole robot.

    What you learned

    ●
    Waypoints make a route

    The robot travels through ordered dots instead of magically knowing where to go.

    ◯
    Lookahead guides each correction

    Pure Pursuit repeatedly chooses a point ahead and steers toward it.

    ⌖
    Localization tells us where we are

    Pinpoint estimates the robot’s X, Y, and heading so the follower can correct its motion.

    ▦
    Safe paths need room

    The route planner must leave enough clearance for the whole robot around the Hive.

    ↔
    Drivetrains move differently

    Mecanum wheels can drive sideways. A tank drive must turn before it can move in a new direction.

    ⌁
    Heading controls where the front points

    A mecanum robot can follow one route while facing a chosen field direction.

    THE FULL ROBOT

    Pathing is one teammate in a bigger system.

    Accurate, simplified map
    1Choose a goal

    Alliance, Hive side, and safe waypoints

    2Know the pose

    Pinpoint measures X, Y, and heading

    3Follow the path

    Pure Pursuit calculates drive corrections

    4Move the chassis

    Mecanum code turns corrections into four motor powers

    ⚙
    What about mechanisms?

    The shooter, intake, transfer, turret, and Limelight aiming are separate systems. A higher-level OpMode or state machine coordinates them with navigation—for example, warming up the shooter while driving, aiming the turret, shooting at the destination, then continuing to park. They do not belong inside the Pure Pursuit math.
